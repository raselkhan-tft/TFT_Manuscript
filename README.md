# Tension Field Theory (TFT)

**A Mechanical Vacuum Research Framework — Rasel Khan**

[Research archive on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/tension-field-theory/) · [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0009-0094-9340) · [Research contact](mailto:Rasel.Khan.TFT@gmail.com)

Tension Field Theory (TFT) is a developing theoretical-physics framework that investigates whether vacuum structure, matter, radiation, gravity, and quantum phenomena can be described through the constitutive mechanics of a structured vacuum.

This repository serves as the manuscript and code hub for the TFT research project, connecting its foundational framework with analytical studies, computational benchmarks, and archived research materials.

## Overview

TFT proposes a conserved, contiguous tension field organized into a **dual-scale, dual-phase architecture**, collectively called the **Spacetime Gravitational Floor (SGF)**:

- **Latent Substrate — Hard Floor:** A phase-locked simple-cubic (SC) structural reference, distinct from ordinary finite-energy propagating modes.
- **Active Vacuum — Soft Floor:** A face-centered-cubic (FCC) nodal scaffold and connected interstitial condensate with distinct roles in restoring response, inertia, pressure constraints, coherent propagation, and localized configurations.

The dual-scale distinction separates latent and active structural organization. The dual-phase distinction separates the nodal scaffold and interstitial condensate within the Active Vacuum.

The research develops explicit mechanical models and examines their correspondence with established physical results. Constitutive assumptions, mathematical consequences, empirical matching, and unresolved physical mechanisms are distinguished in the individual studies.

## Research Status

TFT is an ongoing research program. The proposed vacuum architecture is a constitutive hypothesis; results must be assessed within the assumptions, approximation regimes, and verification scope stated in each manuscript.

Agreement with a benchmark or successful reproduction of a calculation does not, by itself, establish the proposed microscopic constitution of the vacuum. Open questions include the microscopic selection of constitutive laws, the consistent coupling of physical sectors, and their observational consequences.

Archived editions preserve the research record. Subsequent work may refine assumptions, correct inconsistencies, or replace unsuccessful candidates. Preprints and archival deposits should not be confused with peer-reviewed validation, and results from different model versions should not be combined without checking their compatibility.
