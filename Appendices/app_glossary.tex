\chapter*{Glossary of TFT Terminology}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{\protect\numberline{}Glossary of TFT Terminology}
\label{app:glossary}

This glossary defines the principal terms used in Tension Field Theory (TFT).
Definitions follow the manuscript's mechanical conventions and distinguish
foundational postulates, results within specified constitutive closures,
calibrated quantities, and proposed mechanisms. Earlier terminology is
retained where a clarification is needed. For the extended symbol inventory
and units, see the \hyperref[app:notation]{Table of Notation and Constants}.

\begin{description}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0.65\baselineskip}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\emergencystretch}{1.5em}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Absolute Lattice Shear Coupling ($K_{\mathrm L}$).} The energy--length coupling used to express the electromagnetic sector as $K_{\mathrm L}=\alpha\hbar c_{\mathrm E}=k_{\mathrm e}e^2$. Its units are $\mathrm{J\,m}$, not those of a shear modulus. The static geometric estimate and the dynamic FCC-routing ansatz are distinct constructions.
(See \hyperref[app:geometric_alpha]{Appendix~\ref*{app:geometric_alpha}}, \hyperref[app:dynamic_alpha]{Appendix~\ref*{app:dynamic_alpha}} and \hyperref[app:origin_of_charge]{Appendix~\ref*{app:origin_of_charge}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Absolute Substrate Index ($N_{\mathrm S}$).} The dimensionless radial scale ratio $N_{\mathrm S}=R_{\mathrm{Res}}/\ell_{\mathrm S}$. It compares the Structural Reservoir with the substrate spacing; it is not a volume count of all substrate sites or the number of stored records.
(See \hyperref[chap:part3_conclusion]{Chapter~\ref*{chap:part3_conclusion}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Absolute Vacuum.} The State-0 Hard Floor: the latent, phase-locked structural reference beneath the Active Vacuum. ``Absolute'' identifies its reference role and does not describe the finite-energy propagation capacity of the Soft Floor. See \emph{Hard Floor}.
(See \hyperref[part:one]{Part~\ref*{part:one}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Achromatic Scale Relaxation.} The retained limit in which fractional frequency drift is approximately independent of photon frequency over the stated band. Path-based achromaticity concerns the combined integrand $\Gamma_{\mathrm D}/v_{\mathrm{ray}}$, not the rate alone when speed is dispersive. The required frequency dependence must follow from the same projected interaction and propagation model.
(See \hyperref[app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift]{Appendix~\ref*{app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift}} and \hyperref[app:time_dilation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:time_dilation}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Acoustic Transparency.} Earlier terminology for the proposed mechanical interpretation of tunneling. The current construction distinguishes passive evanescent coupling, resonantly enhanced transmission, and externally driven coherent boundary control. None requires the physical barrier to disappear; localized-knot transport additionally uses the stated phase-action guidance closure.
(See \hyperref[app:quantum_tunneling]{Appendix~\ref*{app:quantum_tunneling}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Action and Complete-Cycle Quantization.} The mechanical action associated with an elementary completed excitation satisfies $\oint\pi_q\,dq=h=2\pi\hbar$, where $\pi_q$ is conjugate to the mode coordinate $q$. This is a displacement--momentum integral with units of $\mathrm{J\,s}$, not an energy increment assigned independently to each participating lattice site.
(See \hyperref[app:conservation_action]{Appendix~\ref*{app:conservation_action}} and \hyperref[app:physical_wavefunction]{Appendix~\ref*{app:physical_wavefunction}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Action Intensity ($\rho_\psi$).} The squared magnitude of the action-normalized excitation field, $\rho_\psi=|\psi|^2$. In the continuum normalization, $[\rho_\psi]=\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ and $\hbar\rho_\psi$ is an action density. It is distinct from the structural mass density $\rho$ and from a measured particle probability distribution unless the field--detector correspondence is supplied.
(See \hyperref[app:physical_wavefunction]{Appendix~\ref*{app:physical_wavefunction}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Active Detector Record.} A retained, detector-supported binding configuration with a specified readout tolerance and lifetime. The record may coexist with internal vibration and receiving motion. It is not the same object as the persistent State-0 longitudinal record, and ordinary capture does not require FCC-to-SC saturation.
(See \hyperref[app:deterministic_measurement_resolution]{Appendix~\ref*{app:deterministic_measurement_resolution}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Active Space.} The spatial manifestation of unfolded substrate structure associated with phase variation. The foundational construction introduces the characteristic phase length $x_\phi=\Delta\phi/|\nabla\phi|$ where defined, and distinguishes latent coordinate separation from the strain-weighted propagation path $D_\tau$.
(See \hyperref[part:one]{Part~\ref*{part:one}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Active Topological-Knot Transport.} The continuous motion assigned to a localized matter defect by the phase-action current of a coherently controlled barrier field. In the matched rectangular-barrier construction, reflection cancellation produces a forward current throughout the evanescent region. The trajectory follows only after the matter-field inertia and guidance closure are specified.
(See \hyperref[app:quantum_tunneling]{Appendix~\ref*{app:quantum_tunneling}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Active Vacuum (State I / Soft Floor).} The dual-phase, elastodynamic sector comprising an FCC scaffold and connected Interstitial Condensate. The scaffold supplies contact stiffness and finite kinetic participation; the condensate supplies pressure constraint and localized relative-flow response. Dissipative receiving channels require their own coupling and spectrum. The gravitationally loaded active domain is $0\leq\alpha_{\mathrm G}<1$.
(See \hyperref[part:one]{Part~\ref*{part:one}}, \hyperref[app:fcc_microphysics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:fcc_microphysics}} and \hyperref[app:condensate_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:condensate_impedance}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Added Inertia.} The conservative inertial contribution generated by condensate flow induced by a localized defect. It is obtained from the reversible kinetic energy of the specified flow, rather than from dissipated power. Its value depends on boundary-mode geometry, participating volume, and outer boundary conditions; it need not be a universal scalar for every defect.
(See \hyperref[app:condensate_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:condensate_impedance}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Amplitude Attenuation.} Loss of amplitude from a selected incident mode. For a weak, fixed-frequency harmonic mode with energy-loss coefficient $\gamma_A$, amplitude decays as $e^{-\gamma_A t/2}$ and energy as $e^{-\gamma_A t}$. This does not by itself generate continuous evolution of the surviving carrier's real frequency.
(See \hyperref[app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation}} and \hyperref[app:photon_dispersion_relaxation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:photon_dispersion_relaxation}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Analyzer Response.} The finite mechanical response connecting the prepared pair to recorded analyzer outcomes. It includes orientation-dependent excitation, torque, received action, threshold crossing, and record formation. The Goalpost constraint supplies a shared relation; the detector dynamics and preparation determine the observed statistics.
(See \hyperref[app:quantum_entanglement]{Appendix~\ref*{app:quantum_entanglement}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Angular Coherence.} Preservation of a propagating signal's directional structure and image-forming information. Directional scattering and angular diffusion are separate from mean frequency drift, spectral broadening, and polarization change. Conservation of photon number alone does not guarantee image sharpness.
(See \hyperref[app:time_dilation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:time_dilation}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Angular Standing Modes.} The angular strain patterns on a hydrogenic shell, represented by spherical harmonics with indices $l$ and $m$. The manuscript associates these indices with standing-precession order and projected azimuthal winding, while keeping orbital angular structure distinct from the localized knot's intrinsic spin topology.
(See \hyperref[app:atomic_angular_modes]{Appendix~\ref*{app:atomic_angular_modes}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Areal Boundary Density ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}$).} The inertial mass per area carried by a live FCC/SC boundary, with units $\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}$. For a spherical boundary, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}=M/(4\pi R_{\mathrm{QG}}^2)$. Phase-front conversion, accretion, and tangential redistribution determine its evolution; it is not the volumetric density of the phase-locked interior.
(See \hyperref[app:live_black_hole_boundaries]{Appendix~\ref*{app:live_black_hole_boundaries}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Baryonic Defect Conversion.} The structural-energy allocation in which localized baryonic defects draw a fraction $\beta_{\mathrm b}$ from the unperturbed nodal sector, leaving $\beta_{\Lambda,\mathrm{eff}}=\beta_{\mathrm{geo}}-\beta_{\mathrm b}$. Local condensate participation already included in the coupled baryonic state is not counted again as an independent energy reservoir.
(See \hyperref[app:cosmic_energy_continuum_mechanics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cosmic_energy_continuum_mechanics}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Baryonic Tully--Fisher Relation.} The asymptotic relation $v_\infty^4=G a_{\mathrm{gal}}M_{\mathrm b}$ obtained in the deep, coherent galactic regime when the baryonic exterior field has inverse-square form and the geometric projection approaches unity. The relevant acceleration is $a_{\mathrm{gal}}=a_{\mathrm D}/(2\pi)$, not $a_{\mathrm D}$ itself.
(See \hyperref[app:cosmic_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cosmic_impedance}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Binding Basin.} A region of the detector's mechanical configuration space surrounding a stable minimum $q_j$, with $U'(q_j)=0$ and $U''(q_j)>0$. Capture requires entry into an accessible basin; retention requires sufficient energy export to prevent escape. Finite measurement resolution is a further condition on the residual motion and readout.
(See \hyperref[app:deterministic_measurement_resolution]{Appendix~\ref*{app:deterministic_measurement_resolution}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Bipolar Strain Field.} The coupled strain configuration between the nuclear Torsional Anchor and the electron's time-averaged Resonant Standing-Wave Boundary. It supplies the manuscript's mechanical representation of the internal atomic field, opposite chiral loading, and external neutrality. The central anchor, localized electron, and extended shell field are distinct parts of this description.
(See \hyperref[chap:hydrogen_atom]{Chapter~\ref*{chap:hydrogen_atom}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Bloch Eigenbranch.} A wave-number-dependent family of normal modes of the periodic FCC contact system, obtained from its stiffness and kinetic matrices. Translational, rotational, and phase participation must be determined by the selected eigenvectors and constraints. A mathematical branch is not automatically an independently identified photon, matter, or receiving channel.
(See \hyperref[app:fcc_microphysics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:fcc_microphysics}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Bound-Orbit Strain Correction.} The conservative coupling between radial gravitational strain and tangential transport of a massive bound defect. In the stated weak-field spherical reduction, $\Phi_{\mathrm{orb}}^{(\mathrm{red})}=-GM\ell_{\mathrm{orb}}^2/(c_{\mathrm E}^2r^3)$. It supplies the perihelion correction and remains distinct from galactic condensate loading and optical refraction.
(See \hyperref[app:perihelion_orbital_strain]{Appendix~\ref*{app:perihelion_orbital_strain}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Breathing Mode.} A radial boundary-deformation ansatz for a localized defect supported by an FCC node. The support-scale estimate $f_m=c_{\mathrm E}/(4r_{\mathrm D})$ uses a doubled radial transit. It is not the general proper-time frequency of every massive particle and does not, by itself, derive spin-$1/2$.
(See \hyperref[app:particle_amplitude]{Appendix~\ref*{app:particle_amplitude}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Canonical Momentum ($\pi_q$).} The momentum conjugate to a specified generalized coordinate, defined by $\pi_q=\partial L/\partial\dot q$. For a time-independent harmonic coordinate with kinetic energy $M_q\dot q^2/2$, it is $\pi_q=M_q\dot q$. A time-dependent coordinate constraint can add terms to this relation. The modal canonical momentum is not automatically a localized particle's translational momentum or the momentum density of the complete field.
(See \hyperref[app:physical_wavefunction]{Appendix~\ref*{app:physical_wavefunction}} and \hyperref[app:deterministic_measurement_resolution]{Appendix~\ref*{app:deterministic_measurement_resolution}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Capacity Surface ($r_{\mathrm{cap}}$).} The torsional co-motion surface where the magnitude of the azimuthal Soft-Floor drift equals the local propagation capacity $c_{\mathrm P}$. It is the rotating-model analogue of an ergosurface, not automatically the saturation boundary, photon orbit, or shadow edge.
(See \hyperref[app:kerr_limit]{Appendix~\ref*{app:kerr_limit}} and \hyperref[app:bh_rotating_kerr_clouser]{Appendix~\ref*{app:bh_rotating_kerr_clouser}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Carrier-Projected Spectral Density.} The receiving spectrum weighted by the actual interaction matrix elements and projection onto the photon scale coordinate. It determines access to an energy-transfer channel. It is not the unweighted thermodynamic density of states and therefore does not independently determine thermal multiplicity or heat capacity.
(See \hyperref[app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift]{Appendix~\ref*{app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift}} and \hyperref[app:cmb_thermal_completion]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cmb_thermal_completion}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Catastrophic Decompression (The Big Rupture).} The proposed global phase-reset event in which a connected network of saturated State-II regions loses containment and converts toward the active FCC organization. Interstitial and dynamical freedom are restored. The rupture construction, its threshold, and its chronology depend on the stated macroscopic phase-conversion model.
(See \hyperref[chap:the_big_rupture]{Chapter~\ref*{chap:the_big_rupture}} and \hyperref[app:cosmic_energy_budget_and_chronology]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cosmic_energy_budget_and_chronology}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Center-of-Mass Motion (Boundary Translation).} Translation of the complete black-hole phase domain through the surrounding Active Vacuum. It is one of four distinct boundary-motion channels, alongside normal phase-front growth, tangential redistribution, and torsional motion. An internally phase-locked core need not be a globally immobile object.
(See \hyperref[app:live_black_hole_boundaries]{Appendix~\ref*{app:live_black_hole_boundaries}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Charge and Chiral Integer ($\chi$).} The signed handedness assigned to a persistent localized topological defect. The foundational convention uses $\chi=+1$ for right-handed and $\chi=-1$ for left-handed torsion, with $\chi=0$ for a neutral configuration. This discrete sign is distinct from the dimensional electromagnetic coupling scale $K_{\mathrm L}$.
(See \hyperref[part:one]{Part~\ref*{part:one}} and \hyperref[app:origin_of_charge]{Appendix~\ref*{app:origin_of_charge}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Chiral Nucleation.} The proposed preferential formation of one defect handedness in a torsionally biased decompression state. The nucleation model compares handedness-dependent formation energies and thermal weights. Such a bias is not, by itself, a completed prediction of the cosmological baryon-to-photon abundance.
(See \hyperref[app:baryon_asymmetry]{Appendix~\ref*{app:baryon_asymmetry}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{CHSH Correlation.} The four-setting combination of paired analyzer correlations used to test the entanglement construction. The balanced ideal model gives $E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b$ and a maximal magnitude $2\sqrt{2}$. Gravitational coupling strength, angular ensemble coherence, and normalized recorded correlations are separate quantities.
(See \hyperref[app:quantum_entanglement]{Appendix~\ref*{app:quantum_entanglement}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{CMB Noise Floor.} The ambient fossil-radiation background against which a weak coherent signal is observed. Entering the CMB energy band does not automatically absorb or thermalize a photon, erase its coherence, or terminate propagation. Any terminal assimilation condition requires an independently specified receiving interaction.
(See \hyperref[app:photon_dispersion_relaxation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:photon_dispersion_relaxation}} and \hyperref[app:cmb_thermal_completion]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cmb_thermal_completion}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Coherence Boundary.} The proposed microscopic structural-resolution boundary associated with the FCC node scale $r_{\mathrm D}$. It motivates the manuscript's short-wavelength cutoff discussion. The calibrated length scale and a long-wavelength dispersion expansion do not, by themselves, determine the full-band maximum photon energy or a nonlinear failure threshold.
(See \hyperref[part:one]{Part~\ref*{part:one}} and \hyperref[app:photon_dispersion_relaxation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:photon_dispersion_relaxation}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Coherent Boundary Control.} External control of a barrier's scattering state by a second phase-coherent input. For the symmetric reciprocal two-port construction, incident-side reflection cancels when $a_{\mathrm R}/a_{\mathrm L}=-r_{\mathrm B}/t_{\mathrm B}$. Both the amplitude ratio and phase are fixed, and the outgoing energy includes the contribution supplied by the downstream driver.
(See \hyperref[app:quantum_tunneling]{Appendix~\ref*{app:quantum_tunneling}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Coherent Photon Scale Coordinate.} The dimensionless scale $q$ and logarithmic coordinate $s=\ln q$ used to describe a self-similar photon family. In the retained long-wave construction, energy and frequency scale with $q$, while the packet size scales inversely. The law $\dot s=-\Gamma_{\mathrm D}$ describes motion through that family, not oscillation of a fixed Fourier mode.
(See \hyperref[app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift]{Appendix~\ref*{app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Coherent-Action Preservation.} The constitutive condition that the surviving coherent packet retains $I_\gamma=E_\gamma/\omega_\gamma$ during scale transfer. For the elementary photon, $I_\gamma=\hbar$. Combining this condition with fractional carrier-energy loss gives equal fractional real-frequency loss; ordinary fixed-mode attenuation alone does not supply this condition.
(See \hyperref[app:photon_dispersion_relaxation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:photon_dispersion_relaxation}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Coherent-Photon Energy-Relaxation Rate ($\Gamma_{\mathrm D}$).} The fractional energy-transfer rate of the surviving coherent photon in the scale-relaxation construction. Under coherent-action preservation,
\[
 \frac{\dot E_\gamma}{E_\gamma}
 =\frac{\dot\omega_\gamma}{\omega_\gamma}
 =-\Gamma_{\mathrm D}.
\]
Its units are $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The physical receiving projection remains distinct from the unit-reference numerical evaluation and from detector-induced attenuation.
(See \hyperref[app:photon_dispersion_relaxation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:photon_dispersion_relaxation}} and \hyperref[app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift]{Appendix~\ref*{app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Completed Coordinate Map ($u_i=\mathcal C_i y_i$).} The quasistatic, work-conjugate endpoint map between a presented node--condensate reconfiguration $y_i$ and the completed active contact displacement $u_i$, with $\mathcal C_i=1-\alpha_{\mathrm G}^{(i)}$. The word ``latent'' used for $y_i$ does not identify this active coordinate with independently writable State-0 memory.
(See \hyperref[app:gravitational_endpoint_transfer]{Appendix~\ref*{app:gravitational_endpoint_transfer}} and \hyperref[app:state0_record_writer]{Appendix~\ref*{app:state0_record_writer}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Condensate Entrainment.} The conservative coherent condition $\mathbf v_{\mathrm{IC}}=\dot{\mathbf u}$, so that $\mathbf v_{\mathrm{rel}}=0$. The scaffold and condensate move together; the condensate is not absolutely stationary. This kinematic equality does not independently establish an additional condensate kinetic density to be added to the scaffold mass matrix.
(See \hyperref[app:condensate_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:condensate_impedance}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Constitutive Closure.} A specified relation completing the mechanical response of a retained sector, such as contact isotropy, endpoint work matching, or receiving resistance. A result derived within a closure inherits its regime and assumptions. Symmetry, conservation, and a chosen normalization do not automatically determine every required coefficient or physical interaction.
(See \hyperref[app:fcc_microphysics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:fcc_microphysics}} and \hyperref[app:gravitational_endpoint_transfer]{Appendix~\ref*{app:gravitational_endpoint_transfer}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Contact-Point Relative Displacement ($\Delta\mathbf u_{ij}^{\mathrm c}$).} The objective displacement difference of the material contact points,
\[
 \Delta\mathbf u_{ij}^{\mathrm c}
 =\mathbf u_j-\mathbf u_i
 -r_{\mathrm D}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_i+\boldsymbol\vartheta_j)
       \times\hat{\mathbf n}_{ij}.
\]
It combines translations and contact-frame microrotations. Internal scalar phase differences enter the bond action as separate variables; they are not an extra term in this displacement identity.
(See \hyperref[app:fcc_microphysics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:fcc_microphysics}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Convergence Density ($\rho_{\mathrm{QG}}$).} The scale-transition density used to connect the quantum saturation and gravitational yield constructions. In the retained node-scale model it marks the trigger for loss of active interstitial freedom. It must be distinguished from operational nodal density, the separate gravitational yield density, and the final substrate density.
(See \hyperref[app:phase_transition]{Appendix~\ref*{app:phase_transition}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Convergence Information Limit ($N_{\mathrm{QG}}$).} The dimensionless boundary-capacity normalization
\[
 N_{\mathrm{QG}}=8\pi^2
       \left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{QG}}}{\ell_{\mathrm S}}\right)^2.
\]
It belongs to the manuscript's SC boundary-ledger construction and is distinct from occupied active boundary slots, thermodynamic entropy, and the normalized footprint of a particular localized mass state.
(See \hyperref[app:information_paradox]{Appendix~\ref*{app:information_paradox}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Convergence Radius ($r_{\mathrm{QG}}$).} The microscopic radius associated with the convergence density in the cross-scale phase-transition construction. Lowercase $r_{\mathrm{QG}}$ is not the macroscopic black-hole radius $R_{\mathrm{QG}}$. Its use as a candidate thermal-interface scale does not itself supply a gapless receiving mode or its density of states.
(See \hyperref[app:phase_transition]{Appendix~\ref*{app:phase_transition}} and \hyperref[app:cmb_thermal_completion]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cmb_thermal_completion}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Cosmic Critical Mass ($M_{\mathrm U}$).} The mass assigned to the spherical $R_{\mathrm U}$ domain when its surface potential reaches $GM_{\mathrm U}/R_{\mathrm U}=c_{\mathrm E}^2$, giving $M_{\mathrm U}=c_{\mathrm E}^2R_{\mathrm U}/G$. This is the Type-II capacity construction, conceptually distinct from the black-hole saturation mass and the total Structural Reservoir mass.
(See \hyperref[chap:global_kinematics]{Chapter~\ref*{chap:global_kinematics}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Cosmic Damping Horizon ($R_{\mathrm U}$).} The cosmological name for the Photon Energy-Relaxation Length. In the constant-rate, gravity-neutral reference branch it denotes one energy e-fold, not a material boundary, an automatic photon stopping surface, or the size of the entire Structural Reservoir. See \emph{Photon Energy-Relaxation Length}.
(See \hyperref[chap:cosmic_redshift]{Chapter~\ref*{chap:cosmic_redshift}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Cosmic Impedance ($a_{\mathrm D}$).} The acceleration scale $a_{\mathrm D}=c_{\mathrm E}^2/R_{\mathrm U}=c_{\mathrm E}\Gamma_{\mathrm D}$ for a consistently matched relaxation-rate and length pair. The reduced circular-orbit response uses $a_{\mathrm{gal}}=a_{\mathrm D}/(2\pi)$. The relation does not independently determine the physical numerical value of $\Gamma_{\mathrm D}$.
(See \hyperref[app:cosmic_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cosmic_impedance}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Cubic Contact Isotropy.} The specialization $\kappa_{\mathrm n}=\kappa_{\mathrm t}$ of the objective contact model that produces the selected isotropic leading long-wavelength response. FCC geometry fixes cubic symmetry, not exact elastic isotropy by itself. The coherent pressure constraint and the retained rotational response remain part of the reduction.
(See \hyperref[app:fcc_microphysics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:fcc_microphysics}} and \hyperref[app:condensate_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:condensate_impedance}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Dark Sector.} The TFT allocation of effects conventionally assigned to dark energy and dark matter to structural nodal tension and condensate response. FCC volume fractions describe geometry; interpreting them as energy fractions requires the structural-capacity closure. Galactic excess acceleration additionally requires the extended reactive condensate response, not the void fraction alone.
(See \hyperref[app:cosmic_energy_continuum_mechanics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cosmic_energy_continuum_mechanics}} and \hyperref[app:cosmic_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cosmic_impedance}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Decoherence.} Loss of observable coherent access or reduced ensemble correlation in a specified active system. It may involve receiving interactions, disrupted coupling, or averaging over differing frame orientations. It must not be identified automatically with energy dissipation in each trial or destruction of the persistent Hard-Floor record.
(See \hyperref[app:quantum_entanglement]{Appendix~\ref*{app:quantum_entanglement}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Defect Amplitude ($r_{\mathrm{amp}}$).} The radial boundary-displacement scale used in the thin-layer particle calibration. It is distinct from the common support-node radius $r_{\mathrm D}$. The approximate linear mass--amplitude law requires a specified impedance projection, geometry factor, phase participation, and a sufficiently small ratio $r_{\mathrm{amp}}/r_{\mathrm D}$.
(See \hyperref[app:particle_amplitude]{Appendix~\ref*{app:particle_amplitude}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Defect Impedance Coefficient ($K_{\mathrm D}^{(\mathrm{def})}$).} The thin-layer conservative loading coefficient
\[
 K_{\mathrm D}^{(\mathrm{def})}
 =4\pi r_{\mathrm D}^2\mu_{\mathrm D}

\textbf{True Information Record ($N_{\mathrm M}$).} The mass-state footprint normalization $N_{\mathrm M}=2\pi(m_{\mathrm S}/M)^2$. With the spherical saturation-radius convention it is also $2\pi(\ell_{\mathrm S}/R_{\mathrm{QG}})^2$. It describes the assigned footprint of that state, not a demonstrated count of every stored event or a complete microscopic readout capacity.
(See \hyperref[app:information_paradox]{Appendix~\ref*{app:information_paradox}} and \hyperref[app:graviton]{Appendix~\ref*{app:graviton}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Type I, Type II, and Type III Capacity Limits.} The main text's macroscopic classification: Type I denotes localized gravitational saturation, Type II the cosmological capacity construction associated with $R_{\mathrm U}$, and Type III the microscopic kinematic-resolution limit. These ``Type'' labels are not the material phase labels State 0, State I, and State II.
(See \hyperref[chap:part3_conclusion]{Chapter~\ref*{chap:part3_conclusion}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Uniform Structural-Capacity Closure.} The assumption that the unperturbed nodal and interstitial sectors have the same structural reference energy component per occupied volume. It permits FCC volume fractions to represent structural-energy allocations. It does not require equal operational densities, contact stiffnesses, kinetic participation, or spatial stresses in the two phases.
(See \hyperref[app:cosmic_energy_continuum_mechanics]{Appendix~\ref*{app:cosmic_energy_continuum_mechanics}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Unit-Reference Relaxation Rate ($\Gamma_{\mathrm D}^{(0)}$).} The numerical evaluation obtained by setting $\mathcal F_{\mathrm R}=1$ in the conditional scale-transfer formula, giving approximately $2.36\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ with the retained scale calibration. It is not an independently derived physical nonzero rate, a minimum allowed rate, or a general identification with the observed Hubble slope.
(See \hyperref[app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift]{Appendix~\ref*{app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}

Its numerical equality with the conventional Planck mass follows the adopted substrate-unit normalization. It must not be substituted for an ordinary intrinsic Hard-Floor inertial response, which vanishes in the ideal constraint limit.
(See \hyperref[app:substrate_units]{Appendix~\ref*{app:substrate_units}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Viscous Non-Linear Wave Equation (VNLW).} Earlier terminology for the matter-and-receiving sector. The current treatment separates conservative field dynamics, projected receiving memory, and capture. Its weak, local, narrow-band envelope equation contains $-i\hbar\Gamma/2$ and is linear in the envelope for prescribed coefficients; nonlinear binding and the complete detector dynamics are separate mechanical ingredients.
(See \hyperref[app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Void Exhaustion.} The loss of active interstitial and phase freedom used to characterize approach to the FCC-to-SC transition. It is a finite change of background organization, distinct from the incremental incompressibility imposed on small coherent waves. A tangent incompressibility constraint does not by itself give the nonlinear transition path or trigger kinetics.
(See \hyperref[app:phase_transition]{Appendix~\ref*{app:phase_transition}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Volumetric Yield-Pressure Scale.} The finite structural stress-capacity scale, such as $\tau_{\mathrm S}=c_{\mathrm E}^4/(G\ell_{\mathrm S}^2)$ or $\tau_{\mathrm D,\mathrm{bulk}}=c_{\mathrm E}^4/(Gr_{\mathrm D}^2)$. It has pressure units. A yield capacity is not the incremental bulk modulus or the surface contact coefficient $\tau_{\mathrm D}$.
(See \hyperref[part:one]{Part~\ref*{part:one}} and \hyperref[app:condensate_impedance]{Appendix~\ref*{app:condensate_impedance}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Wave Arrest.} The detector process in which receiving work removes sufficient excess motion for a localized defect to remain in an accessible binding configuration. It is not disappearance of a wave by observation and is not guaranteed by scalar amplitude decay alone. Internal dynamics and finite readout uncertainty may survive after translational capture.
(See \hyperref[app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation}} and \hyperref[app:deterministic_measurement_resolution]{Appendix~\ref*{app:deterministic_measurement_resolution}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Wave-Vector Transport.} Evolution through the real dispersion family required when the surviving photon's frequency changes. In a uniform background, the relaxation contribution must satisfy $\nabla_{\mathbf k}\omega\cdot\dot{\mathbf k}_{\mathrm{rel}}=-\Gamma_{\mathrm D}\omega$. On the nondispersive branch, a direction-preserving specialization gives $\dot{\mathbf k}_{\mathrm{rel}}=-\Gamma_{\mathrm D}\mathbf k$. Recoil and complete energy--momentum balance belong to the same interaction.
(See \hyperref[app:photon_dispersion_relaxation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:photon_dispersion_relaxation}} and \hyperref[app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift]{Appendix~\ref*{app:vacuumdamping_cosmicredshift}}.)

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Weak Force (Torsional Slip).} The unification model's localized axial-torsion reconfiguration channel. The adopted dimensional relation uses $E_{\mathrm{weak}}=2\pi J_i c_{\mathrm E}^2/r_{\mathrm w}$ and $G_{\mathrm F}=E_{\mathrm{weak}}^{-2}$. $G_{\mathrm F}$ is a coupling with inverse-energy-squared units, not a decay rate; rates require the specified transition dynamics.
(See \hyperref[app:unifiedforces_validation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:unifiedforces_validation}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Work Conjugacy.} The pairing of a generalized force with the increment or velocity of its own mechanical coordinate. Coordinate changes must preserve virtual work and consistently transform inertia and momentum. Equal numerical coefficients or a common speed do not establish that two differently normalized coordinates share the same physical impedance.
(See \hyperref[app:gravitational_endpoint_transfer]{Appendix~\ref*{app:gravitational_endpoint_transfer}} and \hyperref[app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation]{Appendix~\ref*{app:matter_vnlw_photon_linear_waveequation}}.)
\end{minipage}

\item[] \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}
\textbf{Zeroth Law of the Tension Field.} The foundational premise of a conserved, contiguous Tension Field from which observable structure and motion arise. Conservation concerns the complete physical content of the coupled system. Reduced coherent modes may lose energy only when the corresponding receiving, interaction, transport, or retained-energy contribution is accounted for elsewhere.
(See \hyperref[part:one]{Part~\ref*{part:one}}.)
