\section{The Standard Entropy Paradox and the Evaporation Fallacy}
In standard General Relativity and Quantum Field Theory, the Information Paradox relies on two assumptions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Holographic Assumption:} The entropy (information) of a black hole is restricted strictly to its surface area via the Bekenstein--Hawking area law:
    \begin{equation}
        S_{BH} = \frac{k_B c_E^3 A}{4G\hbar} = \frac{k_B A}{4\ell_S^2}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Thermal Erasure (Hawking Radiation):} Standard physics posits that black holes possess a non-zero thermodynamic temperature and evaporate over time via random thermal radiation, seemingly deleting the initial information of the collapsed star and violating Quantum Unitarity.
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}
    N_{\text{core}} = \frac{V_{\text{core}}}{\ell_S^3} = \frac{4}{3}\pi \left(\frac{R_{QG}}{\ell_S}\right)^3
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{core}} = k_B N_{\text{core}} \propto R_{QG}^3
\end{equation}

The intrinsic entropy is not thermodynamic (heat), but configurational. Information is stored as discrete Topological Defects within the crystal lattice (mechanically defined as the Graviton strain bits derived in \hyperref[app:graviton]{Appendix \ref*{app:graviton}}). Because the structural yield strength of a single Substrate node ($E_{\text{node}} \approx 10^9 \text{ J}$) exceeds the localized mechanical stress of a single data bit ($E_g \approx 10^{-75} \text{ J}$) by 84 orders of magnitude, the Substrate acts as a perfectly indestructible volumetric ``hard drive'' that cannot be thermally erased or mechanically fractured by the stored data.

\subsection{Observable Entropy (The Holographic Law)}
The event horizon ($R_{QG}$) acts as a phase boundary. External observers cannot probe the volumetric depth of the SC lattice; they can only interact with the surface layer. The observable entropy is limited by the surface node count:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{holo}} = \frac{k_B A}{4\ell_S^2} = \pi k_B \left(\frac{R_{QG}}{\ell_S}\right)^2
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{obs}} = \min(S_{\text{core}}, S_{\text{holo}})

    \item \textbf{For macroscopic black holes ($R_{QG} \gg \ell_S$):} The volume capacity ($R_{QG}^3$) vastly exceeds the surface projection ($R_{QG}^2$), implying:

\begin{equation}
